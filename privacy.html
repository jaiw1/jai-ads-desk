Privacy policy

adsdesk and the Meta app “Jai Ads Manager” · Effective 24 November 2026

The short version

  • adsdesk is open-source software that you install and run on your computer.
  • Your Meta data goes from Meta to your device and nowhere else. Jai Wadhwa, who publishes adsdesk, receives none of it.
  • Your login token is stored on your device, in your operating system’s keychain.
  • A small login relay sees only a one-time login code on its way back to your computer. That code is useless on its own, and the relay stores nothing.
  • There are no accounts to create, no analytics, no tracking, no ads, and nothing is sold.

1. Who we are

adsdesk is published by Jai Wadhwa, a sole proprietor in India whose business is verified by Meta (“Jai”, “we”). adsdesk connects to Meta through the Meta app “Jai Ads Manager” (Meta app ID 1423255306634410), which Jai owns. When you log in, Meta’s login dialog shows that app name.

Contact for anything in this policy, including privacy requests and grievances: jai.w8a@gmail.com.

Jai is the developer of the Meta app and is responsible to Meta for how the app handles data from Meta’s platform. adsdesk is built so that this data stays on your device and never reaches Jai.

2. What adsdesk is

adsdesk is a command-line tool and an MCP server (a way to connect the tool to an AI assistant of your choice). It runs on your computer and talks directly to Meta’s Marketing API, Pages API and Instagram API, using the access you grant when you log in with Facebook Login for Business. The source code will be published at github.com/jaiw1/adsdesk (not live yet).

There is also a browser-based reviewer console, a demo page that lets Meta’s app reviewers try the same features without installing anything. Section 6 covers it.

3. What data adsdesk accesses, and why

Once you approve Meta’s login dialog, adsdesk can reach the ad accounts, Facebook Pages and Instagram accounts that your own Facebook account already has access to, and nothing beyond that. Depending on your account, Meta’s dialog may let you limit which Pages and Instagram accounts are shared. adsdesk only reads or changes the account that you name in each command. 4. Where your data goes and where it is stored

  • From Meta to your device only. Every request goes from your computer straight to Meta (graph.facebook.com) over encrypted HTTPS.
  • Your login token is stored in your operating system’s keychain: macOS Keychain, Windows Credential Manager, or the Linux Secret Service. If your computer has no keychain (for example a server with no desktop), adsdesk warns you and stores it in a file that only your user account can read.
  • Local files. The account list (IDs and the short names you give them), the change plans you review, a log of the changes you approved, and optional Instagram follower snapshots are saved in private files in your user folders. They never leave your device.
  • Jai receives nothing. There is no adsdesk account, server-side database, analytics, crash reporting, telemetry or update check that sends anything to Jai.

The token is never printed on screen, written to logs or included in command output.

5. The login relay

Meta’s login has to return to a fixed web address, but adsdesk waits for the login on a temporary port on your own computer (127.0.0.1). A small relay connects the two. It runs on Cloudflare Workers, and its whole source code is in the project repository (broker/src/index.js).

  • What it sees: the one-time authorization code that Meta sends back after you log in, and the state value adsdesk created (the local port number plus a random check value). Like any website, Cloudflare also handles your IP address and browser details to deliver the request.
  • What it does: it checks the port and immediately redirects your browser to http://127.0.0.1:<port>/callback on your own computer. It never redirects anywhere else.
  • Why the code is useless to it: adsdesk uses PKCE. Turning the code into a token needs a secret “verifier” that adsdesk creates for that single login and that never leaves your computer. adsdesk then swaps the code for a token directly with Meta. The relay never sees your token, and there is no app secret anywhere.
  • What it keeps: nothing. It has no database or storage, it writes no logs, and logging and analytics are switched off for it on Cloudflare.

6. The reviewer console

The reviewer console is a static web page for Meta’s app review and for demos. It logs in with the same Meta app, and then your browser talks directly to Meta.

  • The token is kept only in the page’s memory. It is gone when you close or reload the tab. The page does not use cookies, local storage or any other browser storage.
  • The page loads no scripts from other sites and contains no analytics.
  • It is hosted on GitHub Pages. Like any web host, GitHub receives your IP address and browser details when you load the page; see GitHub’s privacy statement.
  • Meta’s login dialog is Meta’s own page, and Meta’s privacy policy applies to it.

7. AI assistants

If you connect adsdesk’s MCP server to an AI assistant (for example Claude, ChatGPT or another MCP client), the data you ask the assistant to fetch is sent to that assistant’s provider, under your own agreement with them. You choose the provider; Jai does not receive that data and is not a party to that arrangement. adsdesk marks text written by other people (ad copy, captions, posts) so that the assistant treats it as data, not as instructions, and changes still need your exact confirmation.

Jai’s own use. Jai also uses adsdesk himself for businesses that have given him access to their Meta accounts. For that work he uses Claude, an AI assistant made by Anthropic, which processes the results he asks for. This applies only to those businesses’ accounts, not to yours.

8. Sharing and selling

Jai does not sell, rent, trade or share your data, and does not use it for advertising. Because the software sends him nothing, he has no copy of your Meta data to share or to hand over if asked by an authority.

Service providers involved: Cloudflare (runs the login relay; handles the one-time code in transit only) and GitHub (hosts these pages, the reviewer console and the source code). Neither receives your Meta data from adsdesk.

9. How long data is kept

  • Data on your device stays there until you delete it (see how to delete your data).
  • Your login keeps working until you log out or remove the app. Meta stops returning data about 90 days after your last login, until you log in again.
  • If you email Jai, he keeps the email only as long as needed to answer it, and deletes it when you ask unless the law requires him to keep it.

10. Your choices and rights

  • Revoke access from the tool: run meta-control auth logout --revoke. This removes the app’s access on Meta and deletes the token from your device.
  • Revoke access in Facebook: go to Settings & privacy → Settings → Business integrations (or Apps and websites), find “Jai Ads Manager” and choose Remove.
  • Delete local data: see how to delete your data.

Under India’s Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, you have the right to ask for information about your personal data, to have it corrected or erased, to nominate someone to act for you, and to have a grievance addressed. Wherever you live, you can make these requests by emailing jai.w8a@gmail.com. Jai will reply within 30 days. Since Jai does not hold your Meta data, most requests are answered by confirming that and showing you how to delete what is on your own device. If you are not satisfied with the answer, you can complain to the Data Protection Board of India.

11. Security

adsdesk stores tokens in the keychain, uses PKCE so no app secret exists, checks every login with a random state value, talks to Meta only over verified HTTPS, and never prints tokens. Anyone can read the code. To report a security problem, email jai.w8a@gmail.com (see SECURITY.md in the repository). Programs running as your user on your computer can generally read your keychain entries, so keep your computer secure.

12. Children

adsdesk is a business tool for people managing ads and is not meant for anyone under 18. Jai does not knowingly process personal data of children. If you believe a child has used it, email Jai and he will help remove any data involved.
